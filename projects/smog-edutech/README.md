**SMOG EDUTECH** is a STEM/EdTech initiative focused on improving how coding, robotics, digital skills and emerging technology are taught and delivered in schools and community learning environments.

## Recent implementation evidence

A recent 10-month public-school pilot was delivered across **two public schools** and involved **80+ learners**, **24 sessions** and approximately **78% retention**.

The programme combined structured STEM learning with practical coding/robotics activities and provided a real operating environment for testing delivery processes, learner engagement and curriculum progression.

## The systems problem

Good STEM education is not only about having lesson content.

Quality can break down through:
- inconsistent teacher delivery;
- weak progression between lessons;
- poor visibility into learner progress;
- fragmented attendance and reporting;
- curriculum materials that are difficult to update or govern;
- dependence on individual instructors rather than a repeatable operating system.

SMOG is being developed around that wider delivery problem.

## Current product/system direction

### Curriculum system
A structured K–12 progression for coding, robotics, digital citizenship, AI and computational thinking, with Nigerian curriculum requirements treated as a baseline rather than a ceiling.

### Teacher enablement
Training, lesson guidance and delivery support that help teachers understand both what to teach and how to teach it.

### Teacher OS (TOS)
An evolving product concept for managing:
- lesson plans and teaching scripts;
- attendance;
- learner progress;
- portfolio evidence;
- teacher guidance;
- structured curriculum delivery.

TOS is **in development** and should not be interpreted as a fully launched production platform.

### STEM Hub model
A physical learning-centre concept for K–12 and early post-K–12 learners, designed to combine hands-on STEM, coding, robotics and wider digital-skills programmes.

The hub model is also **in development** and is being approached as a staged, practical expansion rather than a claim of an already-built national network.

## Product thinking behind the work

The core design question is:

> How do you make high-quality STEM delivery repeatable even when the quality of individual teachers, schools and environments varies?

That leads to product/system requirements around:
- curriculum progression;
- delivery quality;
- teacher support;
- learner evidence;
- attendance and reporting;
- controlled access to curriculum IP;
- programme measurement;
- school implementation workflows.
